## Book description

*Beyond Apparent Paths: The Practice of Seeking Truth from Facts* is an exploration of how order, life, consciousness, society, and meaning emerge from a world in constant change.

Moving across physics, biology, neuroscience, complexity science, and Eastern and Western philosophy, Guo Jinku develops a unified, multi-level way of understanding reality. The book proposes that disorder is not merely the opposite of order, but the field of possibilities from which order arises; that change is not something added to existence, but the very way existence reveals itself; and that every enduring structure is a temporary achievement sustained within continuous transformation.

Ideas such as entropy, symmetry, emergence, evolution, renormalization, consciousness, and self-organization are presented not as isolated theories, but as different expressions of a shared pattern. Each level of reality possesses its own relative autonomy, yet remains connected to the levels below and above it. Understanding therefore requires more than reduction: it requires knowing when to separate levels, when to connect them, and when to look beyond the categories through which we habitually see the world.

Neither a conventional science textbook nor a purely abstract philosophical treatise, this book is a work of integrative thought. Its central concern is methodological: how to move beyond apparent paths, return to facts and processes, and discover the deeper relations through which the world becomes intelligible.

## The book and the research programme

The book develops the philosophical and methodological perspective of this
collection. It asks how structures take shape, how different levels acquire
their own laws, and how observation, knowledge and action remain grounded in
the processes they describe. Physics, life, mind and society are considered
within this account of change and organization.

The research papers pursue related questions through mathematical constructions,
physical response models and finite computation. The Lean library makes their
formal definitions and proof dependencies explicit; the computational framework
provides executable calculations and their records. These are complementary
ways of developing the concern with facts, processes and relations that runs
through the book.
