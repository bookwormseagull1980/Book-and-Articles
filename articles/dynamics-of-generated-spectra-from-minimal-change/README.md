## Research overview

### A function together with the evidence for its construction

Nonlinear spectral dynamics raises two linked questions. Which regularity survives an interaction, and how many modes are sufficient for a specified observation? This paper answers them through generated function classes. A generated object carries a presentation of its construction and the bounds established along that presentation. These bounds accompany finite operations and admissible limits, making the route to a function available to subsequent analysis.

This perspective adds useful information to a bare space-membership statement. Two presentations may reach the same function while providing different certified bounds. The retained evidence determines which operations and finite approximations have already been justified. The framework therefore connects analytic regularity, construction history and computational resolution.

### Spectral weights must follow the actual interaction

The basic data are a nonnegative self-adjoint generator with compact resolvent and a specified interaction tensor. The tensor records which input modes can generate which output modes. Weighted function classes are tested against this support. A compatible spectral cost must obey the appropriate subadditivity along the interactions that actually occur, while an absolute bilinear majorant controls the size of their coefficients.

This produces a concrete distinction between Gaussian and analytic weights. Heat evolution supplies strong Gaussian damping. Nonlinear multiplication can create a frequency equal to the sum of its inputs, so a quadratic frequency cost need not survive multiplication with the same reserve. A linear analytic cost follows the triangle inequality and can remain compatible with the generated frequencies. The paper determines the relevant weight regimes in Neumann cosine models and in derivative-losing Stokes-Leray realizations.

Regularity is thus propagated through the generation rule itself. The interaction support decides the available estimate, and the estimate records the amount of regularity used by the operation.

### Nonlinear evolution with a finite-window certificate

The mild equation combines semigroup smoothing with the interaction bound. The derivative loss is absorbed through an integrable time singularity, giving local existence and a common interval of control for the spectral cutoffs. Compatible approximations preserve the relevant source bounds and the generated presentations. Positive-time analytic estimates describe the regularity available after the evolution begins.

A gap between stronger and weaker spectral weights controls both the initial discarded tail and the nonlinear source omitted by a finite projection. A residual estimate and a Volterra stability argument then bound the discrepancy between a finite Galerkin trajectory and the complete evolution on the common interval.

An observation enters through its continuity modulus. Given a requested observation accuracy, the estimates identify the least cutoff certified by the available bounds. This turns the infinite spectral problem into a finite-window task whose adequacy is demonstrated mathematically.

### Quadratic accounts and the return of history

The paper also develops quadratic ledgers for composed constructions. Positive transport defects and signed injections are recorded separately, with their full cross terms. Telescoping identities preserve their joint contribution through successive operations, and admissible limits retain the needed bounds. This is particularly useful when separate estimates of individual pieces would discard the cancellation or compatibility of the complete expression.

The history-resolved arithmetic construction supplies a second application. Its recursive probabilities normalize the source on each expression fibre, so history multiplicity does not become an artificial factor in the analytic estimate. The assembly operator has a Gaussian-controlled trace tail. The paper gives a criterion for its readout to close as an autonomous value evolution. When history differences couple back to the values, exact elimination produces a memory term instead.

These results connect generation records to dynamics in a precise way. A history can carry the evidence needed for an approximation and can also carry hidden state that affects later observations. The analysis distinguishes those roles while using the same finite and infinite spectral machinery.

### The main advance

The paper establishes interaction-compatible regularity classes, local nonlinear evolution with cutoff-uniform control, finite-window observation bounds and compositional quadratic accounts. It also connects history assembly to reduced dynamics through an explicit closure criterion and memory formula.

The distinctive result is the passage from a specified generating interaction to a certified finite observation. The construction determines the admissible weight, the evolution propagates its bound, the tail estimate controls what is omitted, and the observation modulus fixes the necessary resolution. This provides an analytic foundation for finite spectral computation with a traceable error budget.
