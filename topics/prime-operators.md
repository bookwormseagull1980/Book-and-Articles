# Prime Decomposition and Operator Responses

Prime factorization is studied on the generated arithmetic family and on the
linear spaces induced by it. The point is to retain the relation between
algebraic irreducibility, decomposition histories and operator localization.

## Three linked descriptions of prime directions

Prime modes are the nonunit elements irreducible under cascade composition.
Their residue classes form the canonical basis of the augmentation cotangent
space. The corresponding one-word bar vectors span the degree-one kernel of
the decomposition Hodge operator. These are identifications between the
appropriate sets, quotients and subspaces.

On the mode indexed by a positive integer n greater than one, the degree-one
operator has eigenvalue d(n)-2, where d(n) is the number of positive divisors.
Its kernel is therefore precisely the prime subspace. The associated heat
projection has sharp operator-norm error exp(-tau); the resolvent supplies a
second quantitative prime projector.

## Occupation, parity and Gaussian scale

Unique factorization gives prime-occupation coordinates. Symmetric occupation
traces recover the Euler product for zeta, while graded exterior traces recover
its reciprocal in the domain of absolute convergence. Square-free selection
and exterior parity realize the Moebius coefficients. These occupation spaces
are canonically linked to the natural-mode family.

Creating a prime direction changes both occupation parity and quadratic
Gaussian scale. Exact signed dilation identities retain both effects. Finite
prime-direction transfers preserve the complete two-parity response before
an unsigned capacity bound is taken.

## Relative inputs survive addition

Addition merges several ordered input pairs into a single output value.
A faithful additive dilation records the relative input in a defect space.
The central-parity intertwiner expresses precisely how multiplicative parity
is transported through this addition map. Common-factor and primitive-pair
decompositions give a second interface between additive observation and
multiplicative generation.

These operators and identities make it possible to formulate questions about
the complete signed scale response without discarding signs or cross terms.
The counting applications recover Bertrand's theorem and the prime-number
asymptotic within the constructed system. The Riemann-hypothesis research
interface concerns uniform control of the full signed response; the identities
and prime projectors specify the objects on which such an estimate must act.
